Stop apps from burning your hotspot data

Hotspot Guard is a macOS menu bar tool. It watches how much data each app pulls over your iPhone hotspot, and steps in when one of them runs away with it.

Download for macOS

Version 1.2.0 · 1.1 MB · macOS 14 or later (Apple silicon)
Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple — opens without warnings.
1.2.0 adds plan profiles: keep a separate monthly ledger for each connection you use — a home phone’s 30 GB and a travel eSIM’s 5 GB are different budgets, and the guard remembers which profile belongs to which connection. One profile stays free forever; multiple profiles are the paid Plus unlock described below.
1.1.1 adds an optional online lookup for apps the built-in pack does not know. It is off until you allow it, sends only the one process name you click Explain on — never automatically — and answers are cached on your Mac.
1.1.0 explains what you are looking at. Click any app in the panel and it tells you what that process is, why it uses data, and whether limiting it is safe — from a built-in knowledge pack covering 149 common apps and system daemons, so it works fully offline. A new per-app detail view also shows exactly what the guard did and why: how often it stepped in, what those retries cost, and which rule fired. Entries written by AI and not yet human-reviewed say so on the label.
1.0.14 puts the app version into the address the opt-in update check calls, so a release with a defect can be spotted from the server side even when nobody writes in. The request still carries only the app version and your macOS version — the same two facts as before, in one more place.
1.0.13 stops the guard from wasting data on apps that come straight back after being stopped. System updaters restart automatically and begin their download over, so stopping them again and again cost more data than it saved — measured live: one updater retried 21 times in a morning, losing about 20 MB per round. Now the third strike within six hours freezes the app instead: a frozen app is not restarted and keeps its download progress, so the transfer finishes in a few paced steps. A notification shows what the retries cost, with one-tap ways out.
1.0.12 adds an optional update check. It asks once, inside the menu panel, and does nothing until you say yes. If you allow it, once a day the app asks this site whether a newer version exists, sending only the app version and your macOS version — no account, no usage data, no app names. Say no and it never connects. This exists because there was previously no way to tell you a fix had shipped, and ten builds have been pulled for real defects. 1.0.12 also fixes the tether hint coming back after every relaunch once dismissed.
1.0.11 actually fixes per-app measurement. 1.0.10 was released claiming to fix it and did not: on a phone hotspot the list still read “No traffic yet” while data was being used, even though the monthly total kept climbing. Each sample was doing a name lookup that stalled on hotspot DNS and got cut off before it returned any data. A sample round now takes about 0.3 seconds instead of five. 1.0.8 stopped the menu bar icon from showing full Wi-Fi while you are offline, and tells you when your iPhone is plugged in but macOS is still sending traffic over Wi-Fi.
The guard itself is free, forever — everything above, no time limit.
Hotspot Guard Plus ($24.99, one-time) adds plan profiles: separate monthly ledgers for each connection you juggle, with one-click switching. Get Plus — your license key arrives by email; activate it in Settings → About (works on up to 3 Macs). See the terms of sale before buying.

Per-app limits, not all-or-nothing

Set how much an app may use in a minute or an hour. Apps keep working normally until they cross the line.

Warns before it acts

Highlight, notify, ask, or step in automatically — you choose the level for each rule. Automatic action is off by default.

Knows when you are on the hotspot

It only guards on metered connections and goes idle on regular Wi-Fi, so it never gets in your way at home.

Ignores local traffic

AirDrop, Bonjour, and anything on your local network never counts against your plan, because it never touched the hotspot.

Keeps big downloads off the hotspot

Mark Xcode, Docker, or any command line tool as blocked, and it asks you the moment they reach for the network.

Monthly budget with a forecast

See what is left in your plan and whether you are on track to run out before the billing period ends.

Hotspot Guard runs entirely on your Mac. It has no account and no usage telemetry: what it measures — per-app traffic, budgets, the knowledge pack, and the record of what it did — never leaves your machine. It reaches the network in only three places, and two of them are off until you say yes: the once-a-day update check (app version and macOS version only) and the click-to-explain lookup for an unknown process. The third applies only if you buy Plus, which checks your license key. The privacy policy spells out each one.
